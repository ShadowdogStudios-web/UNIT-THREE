1 What does this photograph show about the Gilded Age?

Why: Breaker boys picked slate out of coal by hand for long shifts. Hine's photos were evidence in the fight against child labor.

2 What did Carnegie mean?

“The man who dies thus rich dies disgraced.”

Andrew Carnegie, “Wealth” (The Gospel of Wealth), 1889

Why: Carnegie's 'Gospel of Wealth' said the rich had a duty to use their money for public good, such as libraries.

3 Social Darwinism was used to argue that…

Why: Social Darwinism took a false idea about nature and used it to excuse poverty and inequality.

4 In this cartoon, giant money bags labeled with trust names stand over the U.S. Senate. What is the cartoonist arguing?

Why: Keppler shows the trusts as the real 'bosses' of the Senate, standing above the lawmakers.

5 This is an example of…
By 1880, Standard Oil controlled about 90 percent of the oil refining in the United States.

Why: When one company controls almost all of a product, it is a monopoly and can set prices.

6 What was the goal of the Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890?

Why: The law banned combinations that restrained trade. At first it was weakly enforced — and was even used against unions.

7 Why did workers in Pullman, Illinois, go on strike in 1894?

Why: During a depression, George Pullman cut wages while keeping rents in his company town the same.

8 How did the federal government respond to the Pullman Strike?

Why: A federal injunction and U.S. troops broke the strike. Eugene V. Debs was jailed for defying the injunction.

9 The Haymarket rally on May 4, 1886, was called to…

Why: The rally followed police killings of strikers at the McCormick Reaper Works the day before.

10 How did the American Federation of Labor (AFL) differ from the Knights of Labor?

Why: The Knights tried to unite nearly all workers. The AFL, led by Samuel Gompers, organized skilled workers for practical gains.

11 When workers negotiate with an employer as one group, it is called…

Why: Collective bargaining gives workers power they do not have one at a time.

12 What was the biggest reason Chicago grew so fast in the late 1800s?

Why: As the railroad hub of the continent, Chicago connected Western farms and ranches to Eastern cities.
